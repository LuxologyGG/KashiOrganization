# Open questions for Arash

Everything in the project stories is published in his own words. Before this goes live, a few things need his
call. They are ordered by how much they block.

Each project was independently checked against LA County Assessor records, City of Palos Verdes Estates
planning files, and MLS aggregators. Where the record and the story disagree, we have left **his** version in
place and flagged it here rather than quietly rewriting it.

---

## 1. Blocking: The Lockdown House has the wrong link

The document gives this project the same Redfin URL as The Glass House
(412 S Francisca Ave, **Redondo** Beach). That is a different property: 6 bedrooms, sold 2025. The Lockdown
House is described as a 4 bedroom in **Manhattan** Beach that listed the week before the March 2020 shutdown.

We searched 14 sources and could not identify it. Worth knowing: the public permit trail for Kashi
(CSLB #998954) shows Hermosa Beach projects and no Manhattan Beach ones, and the "322 Manhattan Avenue"
project is itself in Hermosa Beach, which is an easy thing to trip on.

Already ruled out, so no need to re-check: 501 18th St, 316 28th St, 1626 1st St.

**Ask:** the MLS number, the listing agent, or the correct Redfin/Zillow link. Any one resolves it in a single
lookup. Also worth confirming: is the city Manhattan Beach, or was it Hermosa?

**Until then:** the story is published with no address, no specs and no record link. Nothing has been guessed.

## 2. Two places where the record disagrees with the story

**The Glass House, the house that was demolished.** The story describes bringing down "the final three-story
wing of the original 100-year-old house." The county record shows the prior structure was built in **1912 as a
single-story residence**, converted to a **duplex in 1959**, and was a two-unit, two-level building
(2 bed / 2 bath, 1,968 sq ft) when it was acquired in March 2022.

The age is fine and actually understated, 1912 to 2022 is about 110 years. It is the "three-story house" part
that does not match. A version that stays true and survives scrutiny: *"replacing a 1912 duplex that had stood
on the site for over a century."*

**Ask:** is the three-story detail a memory of the new build rather than the old one, or was there an addition
the record does not capture?

**The Brick House, "we waited three years for the lot."** That reads as vacant land, but the City of Palos
Verdes Estates Planning Commission minutes for applications NC-1637-18 and G-1627-18 both describe work
*"at the single family residence located at 2245 Via La Brea,"* and the filed roof plan marks an existing deck.
So a house stood on the parcel at the 2018 acquisition. The record cannot tell us whether it was fully
demolished or heavily added onto.

**Ask:** which was it? It changes whether the copy can say "built from the ground up."

Good news on the same project: **the never-sold claim checks out.** The Prop 13 base years split (land 2018 by
transfer, improvements 2021 by new construction), which only happens when a parcel is bought, built on, and
never resold. "It was built for us" is solidly supported.

## 4. Photography: we found the eight in the document, and we need the originals

The Word document had **eight photos embedded in it**, which we extracted and put on the site. They cover exactly
the three projects that had nothing:

| Project | Photos now live |

Two useful things came out of them. The Glass House photo clearly shows the **floating staircase**, which no
public record mentioned, so that detail in the story is now backed by an image. And the Dutch door photo shows
the **1801 address numbers**, confirming the Modern Farmhouse address.

**The problem: they are screenshots, not photographs.** Five were taken from a photo viewer and three from a
browser on the Redfin listing, so they carry that window's chrome (now cropped off) and they cap out at roughly
1400 x 950 pixels, some smaller. That is fine mid-page. It is thin for a full-width header on a large monitor.

**The screenshots show the real files exist.** One title bar reads `412SFrancisca_HiRes (10 of 99).jpg`, and the
Redfin viewer shows "2 OF 55".

**Ask:** the actual folders, not more screenshots. Specifically the `412SFrancisca_HiRes` set (99 files), the
equivalent for 1801 6th Street, and whatever exists for The Lockdown House and The Brick House. A shared drive
link or a WeTransfer is easiest. Anything at 2000 px wide or better is ideal.

**Priority:** The Brick House first, since it is the only project still on stand-in photography and the view and
the white brick are the entire story. Then higher-resolution versions of the three above.

One legal note worth a sentence from him: the three Modern Farmhouse images came off the Redfin listing, and
listing photos are normally the photographer's or the brokerage's copyright rather than the builder's. He very
likely has rights to photography of his own project, but it is worth confirming before launch.

## 5. Small details we could not source

None of these block launch. They sit inside his stories, which are presented as his account, so they read fine.
They would only need documenting if he wants them stated as specifications.

- **"Floating staircase"** appears in the stories for the Glass House, the Modern Farmhouse and the Lockdown
  House. It is documented only for South of the Pier ("floating stairs with glass railings" in the MLS copy).
  A photo of each would settle it.
- **The Brick House, "custom Arto brick molded for the project."** No manufacturer is named in the filed plans,
  which describe a whitewashed brick veneer. An invoice or spec sheet would let us state it outright.
- **The Brick House, "48-foot sliding glass wall."** The filed plans show pocket French doors, and the longest
  dimensioned upper-level run is 42 feet 8 inches. The as-built door schedule would settle it.
- **The Brick House, "vertical cedar siding."** Vertical siding is confirmed; the only species named in the
  plans is redwood.
- **The Glass House, "Fleetwood window and door package."** Only the doors are documented.
- **The Glass House, "sold to a family."** Buyer identity is not publicly sourceable, and it is privacy
  sensitive. We would suggest keeping it as the warm, non-specific line it already is.

Two credits we can add if he wants them, both well sourced but neither mentioned in his stories:

- **The Brick House** was designed by **Joseph Fournier Design, Inc.** (named in the PVE planning minutes and on
  the stamped plan sheets). Note it is a design firm, so "designed by" is safer than "architect."
- **The Glass House** MLS copy reads *"A Kashi Organization development and Louie Tomaro design,"* so Tomaro can
  be credited there too, not only on South of the Pier.

On South of the Pier we credit **Tomaro Design Group**, the firm, because that is what the MLS confirms. Louie
Tomaro personally as architect of record is not confirmed for that address.
